Conveyancing

What is conveyancing?

Conveyancing is the legal process involved in transferring ownership of immovable property. A conveyancer assists with the legal documents, communication with relevant parties and registration-related steps.

What documents do I need for a property transfer?

The required documents depend on the transaction. Common documents may include identity documents, the signed sale agreement, bond details, rates clearance information, FICA documents and any supporting property documents requested by the conveyancer.

How long does a property transfer take?

The timeline depends on the transaction, bond approval, municipal clearance, Deeds Office processes and whether all parties provide the required documents timeously. The firm should explain the expected process and keep clients updated as the matter progresses.

When should I contact a conveyancing attorney?

It is best to contact a conveyancing attorney as early as possible, especially once a property transaction is being negotiated or an offer to purchase is signed. Early guidance can help avoid delays and misunderstanding.

Estates

Do you assist with deceased estates?

Yes. The firm provides guidance with reporting deceased estates, applications to the Master of the High Court, estate documentation, asset collection and distribution steps, and the administration process from start to finish.

How soon must a deceased estate be reported in South Africa?

In South Africa, deceased estates are generally reported to the Master of the High Court within 14 days of the date of death. Clients should seek guidance early because the required documents and process can differ depending on the circumstances.

What documents are usually needed for deceased estate administration?

Common documents may include the death certificate, identity documents, the will if one exists, marriage certificate or marital status information, asset and liability details, bank information and any documents requested by the Master of the High Court.
